### L/R side classification on OC1

**Roll rate doesn't work** because the ama suppresses roll. **Yaw rate (gyro_z)
does** — a LEFT stroke applies forward thrust offset to the left of
centerline, yawing the bow RIGHT (negative yaw rate). At the per-lap level,
band-pass yaw at 0.02-0.15 Hz, sample the envelope sign per stroke catch,
and report the fraction of time on each side. Per-stroke labels with
hysteresis (k=3) get the run-length distribution into the user's stated
"8-15 strokes per side" range about half the time. Choppy water makes
per-stroke labels noisy; lap-level fractions remain solid.

### User lean toward the ama

The user paddles leaned ~5-15° toward the LEFT (ama) side. This shows up as
a persistent positive offset on the lateral accel axis (gravity projects
~1-2 m/s² onto +y when the boat is rolled left). When using lateral accel as
a feature, **always demean per-lap or subtract `g·sin(estimated_lean)`** to
remove this bias.

### Force-curve windowing artifact

Stroke segments are defined midpoint-to-midpoint between consecutive
catches. So each window includes the tail of the previous glide (negative
force) → pull (positive) → start of next glide (negative). The negative
tails are real and informative — they show drag during recovery — but they
can confuse a first-time viewer. The PM5-strict plot (`20_perg_pm5_strict.png`)
clips to positive only for a cleaner stroke-shape view.

### The Connected % metric (added after the initial handoff was written)

Beyond stroke detection and side discrimination, the pipeline now reports
`connected_fraction` per lap — the fraction of strokes whose positive-only
force curve has only ONE peak (a textbook PERG arch) vs a separate small
catch shoulder followed by a lull followed by the main drive.

Implementation in `correlate_kg_garmin.py::_is_connected_stroke`:
- Resample stroke segment to 101 phase points, clip to ≥ 0 N.
- `scipy.signal.find_peaks` with absolute height floor (10 N) and
  prominence scaled to each stroke's amplitude (5%).
- 1 peak → "connected" (single arch). >1 peak → "not connected" (visible
  catch bump + lull + drive).

Session 37 numbers (use as baselines when comparing to future sessions):
- Lap 13 (glass-water mile, slow current): **18% connected** at 48.6 spm.
- Lap 2 (chop mile, strong push): 6% connected at 47.4 spm.
- Lap 1 (warmup, low cadence): 35% connected at 41.7 spm.
- Across all lap-2-and-lap-13 strokes, when there IS a catch shoulder
  the drive dominates ~5:1 and the lull is only ~9-13% of drive peak.
  Real bump-lull-drive structure, not a dramatic disconnect.

### What drives Connected % — chop vs effort vs cadence

A regression on session-37 lap-level data (`23_chop_vs_connection.png`)
ranked three hypotheses against Connected %:

| Hypothesis | Pearson r |
|---|---|
| Chop level (lateral RMS 0.2-0.5 Hz) | −0.47 |
| Mean peak drive force per lap | +0.03 |
| Cadence (spm) | −0.56 |

**Cadence is the biggest single driver.** Pulling hard isn't the problem
(near-zero correlation with force). Chop has a moderate effect. The
mechanism is biomechanical: higher cadence shrinks the time between
recovery and catch, so the body engages a beat late and the catch
shoulder shows up. The takeaway when interpreting future sessions:
- Compare Connected % within the same cadence band.
- Don't be alarmed by low Connected % in strong-effort/high-cadence
  laps — that's expected.
